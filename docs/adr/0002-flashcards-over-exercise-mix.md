# Self-graded spaced-repetition flashcards instead of a Duolingo-style exercise mix

Status: accepted; the self-grading part is superseded by ADR 0003.

The original idea was a game-style app with mixed exercise types (multiple choice, match pairs, listen-and-pick). We chose flashcards scheduled by FSRS instead: English → say the Korean aloud → reveal → self-grade, plus a separate Listen Direction. A single listen-and-pick check is kept, and only in the Learn session. The research is consistent that recall-type practice beats recognition-type practice for retention (Rowland 2014; Kang et al. 2007), and that practising English → Korean builds speaking ability (Webb 2009), which is what a traveller needs. Multiple-choice lures can plant errors (Roediger & Marsh 2005), and match-pairs can be solved by elimination. Gamification shows small, fragile effects on learning (Sailer & Homner 2020), so it is kept to a thin layer (XP, Streak, Stars, stats) on top of the loop rather than shaping the exercises.

**Addendum (2026-09-27):** Illustrations were added for every Expression, and a third Direction, See, was added: the picture is the prompt and the learner says the Korean. The research on pictures is mixed. Pictures don't beat translations (Carpenter & Olson 2012), and they inflate confidence. So Speak keeps the English prompt, Learned stays based on Speak only, and Illustrations never appear on Speak or Listen prompts. The learner chose See on every Expression, including ambiguous pictures. At the user's request See is a checked choice rather than self-graded: pick the matching Korean from four options, then hear it. That makes See a recognition exercise, which is weaker for retention than recall but can't be graded wrongly. Recall is still covered by Speak. Expressions that share a drawing are never offered as distractors.
